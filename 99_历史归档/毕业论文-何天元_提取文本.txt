Implementation Feasibility and Preliminary Effectiveness of Digital Monitoring-Assisted Resistance TrainingAuthor: He TianyuanSupervisor: Prof. Sun Jian June, 2027
Objective: In resistance training, digital monitoring tools are increasingly applied, yet empirical evidence remains limited regarding their incremental value over standardized self-guidance and the measurement foundation of low-cost mobile solutions. This study employed a three-phase sequential design
instrument validation, pilot randomized controlled training comparison, and semi-structured interviews
to systematically evaluate the implementation feasibility, training execution characteristics, exploratory training effects, and user acceptance of digital monitoring-assisted resistance training in a general-population lower-body resistance training context, and to provide effect size estimates and process optimization parameters for subsequent definitive randomized controlled trials.Methods: Study 1 recruited 13 healthy males with training experience to collect synchronized barbell back-squat mean concentric velocity data from a self-developed mobile system (SportSci Pro) and GymAware linear position transducer, as well as jump height data from the mobile system against 240 fps high-speed video manual annotation, under controlled conditions. Intraclass correlation coefficients, Bland-Altman analysis, and standard error of measurement were used to assess measurement agreement. Study 2 employed a two-arm parallel, open-label, stratified block-randomized controlled pilot design. Thirty-six healthy young male trainees who completed randomization (AI-assisted group n=18, self-guidance group n=18) underwent a 4-week, twice-weekly lower-body resistance training intervention. Qualitative interviews revealed that digital monitoring's value may manifest as reduced load-selection uncertainty, provision of safety boundaries, and enhanced training control perception, though operational experience and feedback simplicity require improvement.Conclusion: The digital monitoring-assisted training protocol demonstrates preliminary implementation feasibility in a 4-week general-population lower-body resistance training context. The mobile system shows favorable measurement agreement in the limited paired data, with positive changes observed in training self-efficacy. However, current evidence is insufficient to establish superiority of digital monitoring over standardized self-guidance in maximal strength or power outcomes.
